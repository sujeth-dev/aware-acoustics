===== SLIDE 9 (name=Slide7) =====
  | Page 10
  | Reverberation Time (RT60)
  [IMAGE] media/image4.jpg  (4.65104in x 4.06597in)
  ~ Ta = 0.16 V/A
  ~ Where
  ~ Ta = reverberation time(s)
  ~ V = room volume (m3)
  ~ A = the total sound absorption of the room (m2 Sabine)
  ~ Reverberation Time (RT) is the duration required for sound energy in an enclosed space to decay by a specified amount after the sound source is abruptly stopped. It is typically measured as a 60 dB reduction, referred to as RT60.
  ~ RT60 is a widely accepted acoustic parameter used to characterize the acoustic quality of spaces such as concert halls, auditoriums, and performance venues, indicating how long sound persists before diminishing to 60 dB below its initial level.

===== SLIDE 12 (name=Slide11) =====
  | Page 13
  | Articulation Loss
  [IMAGE] media/image10.png  (5.29514in x 3.90972in)
  ~ Articulation Loss of Consonants (ALCONS) represents the percentage of consonants that are not correctly perceived by a listener, indicating speech clarity and intelligibility within a space.
  ~ An ALCONS value of0%denotes excellent speech intelligibility with no consonant loss. Values around10%indicate reduced clarity and progressively poorer intelligibility, while15%is generally considered the upper limit of acceptable articulation loss.
  [IMAGE] media/image11.png  (6.80729in x 1.70139in)
  ~ Background Noise:Ambient noise can mask consonant sounds, reducing speech clarity and making words harder to distinguish.
  ~ Reverberation:Excessive reverberation or echoes smear speech over time, particularly affecting the intelligibility of consonants.
  ~ Speaker Quality:Speakers with weak vocal projection or poor articulation typically result in higher ALCONS values.
  ~ Listener Hearing:Individuals with hearing loss or reduced speech perception ability are more likely to experience increased articulation loss.

===== SLIDE 14 (name=Slide20) =====
  | Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC)
  | Page 15
  ~ The Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC) is a standardized, single-number rating used to evaluate the sound absorption performance of acoustic materials. It represents the average sound absorption coefficient measured at mid-frequency octave bands.
  ~ NRC values range from 0.0 to 1.0, with higher values indicating greater sound absorption and reduced sound reflection within an enclosed space.
  ~ NRC 0.0: No effective sound absorption; sound energy is fully reflected.
  ~ NRC 0.5: Moderate absorption, with approximately 50% of incident sound energy absorbed.
  ~ NRC 1.0: High absorption, with most incident sound energy absorbed and minimal reflection.
  ~ Measurement Standards: NRC values are typically determined in accordance with ASTM C423 / ISO 354, based on laboratory measurements conducted in a reverberation chamber.
